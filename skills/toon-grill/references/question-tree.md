# Question tree for one scene

Walk it top-down. Each level depends on the ones above. Skip what the files already answer, and say where you took the answer from.

## 1. Point of the scene

- What changes between the start and the end of the scene? Ask for one sentence.
- Whose scene is it? Whose reaction does the audience watch?
- What must the audience understand or feel by the last shot?

## 2. Space and starting positions

- Where is each character at the first frame? Ask about the left or right of frame, near or far, and what they face.
- Who enters or leaves, from where and when?
- Which landmarks of the location does the scene use, and where are they relative to the characters?
- Does the scene break the 180-degree line on purpose? If not, which side is the camera on?

## 3. Props and hands

This is where generations fail most, so be exact.

- Which props are in the scene, and where is each at the start?
- For every prop: who picks it up, in which shot and with which hand? How is it held: one hand or two, by which end?
- Who puts it down or hands it over, and where does it end up?
- Is a prop in Blender named as a prop? If the file check lists it as a traveller, settle that now.

## 4. Action and acting per shot

- What does each character do in this shot? Ask for one main action.
- How do they feel, and how does it show in body and face?
- Do they turn, and to where? This matters because the video model can turn characters only when the text asks.
- Where do they look: at whom, or at what off screen?
- Is there speech? Without generated audio, describe the mouth and gesture, or a reaction instead.

## 5. Shots and timing

- Does every story beat have a shot? Is any shot doing two jobs?
- How long is each shot? Flag shots under half a second, and decide whether they stay.
- Which cuts continue one action across the cut? Those become sticky sequence boundaries.
- Do close-ups and wide shots of the same action agree on positions and props?

## 6. Camera

- What must each camera show? Who, and how big in frame?
- Which shots move the camera, and why? Moves come from Blender, so check that the layout has them.
- Is there anything the camera must not show, such as a spoiler or an unfinished set?

## 7. Off screen

- What happens between the shots that the audience must assume?
- Is there a time skip inside the scene? It explains a position jump across a cut, so record it.

## 8. End state

- Where is everyone at the last frame, what do they hold, and what changed about them?
- What state are the props and the set in? This becomes the next scene's start.

## 9. Style and limits

- Anything this scene must avoid, such as gore, extra characters or text on screen?
- Anything it must match from an earlier episode?
